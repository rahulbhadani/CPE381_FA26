\section{Linear Interpolation (6 Points)}
Assume three signal values  $x = \{3.0, 4.0, 3.5\}$ at timestamp $t = \{ 2.0, 4.0, 6.0\}$.  We will consider the case of linear interpolation.

\begin{enumerate}
\item How many linear segments are required if given three samples or signal values? \textbf{(2 Points)}
\item Write down all the equation of line segments necessary for linear interpolation given the three samples above. \textbf{(2 Points)}
\item  What is the value of when interpolated at $t = 2.5$?  \textbf{(2 Points)}
\end{enumerate}


\begin{answerbox}
\begin{enumerate}
\item 2

\item

The Equation of line connecting two points are

\begin{multiequation}
\cfrac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1} = \cfrac{x - x_1}{t  - t_1}
\end{multiequation}

The equation of the first line segment is :


\begin{multiequation}
\cfrac{4 - 3}{4 - 2} & = \cfrac{x - 3}{t  - 2}\\
\frac{1}{2} & = \frac{x - 3}{t - 2}\\
t - 2 & = 2x - 6\\
t & = 2x - 4\\
x & = \frac{1}{2}(t + 4)
\end{multiequation}

The equation of the second line segment is:

\begin{multiequation}
\cfrac{3.5 - 4}{6 - 4} & = \cfrac{x - 4}{t  - 4}\\
\frac{-0.5}{2} & = \frac{x - 4}{t - 4}\\
-(t-4) & = 4x - 16\\
x & = \frac{1}{4}(20 - t)
\end{multiequation}


\item Since $t = 2.5$ is between $2.0$ and $4.0$ we will plug its value into the first segment.

\begin{multiequation}
x = 0.5*(2.5 + 4) = 3.25
\end{multiequation}


\end{enumerate}

\end{answerbox}

\section{Cubic Spline Interpolation (10 Points)}

Consider signal values $x = \{0.0, 7.0, 9.0, 3.0, 4.0\}$ at timestamp $t = \{ 0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 5.0\}$.  We will consider the case of cubic spline interpolation.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Assuming free boundary condition, write the $B$ matrix corresponding to all the $b_i$ coefficients as per formulation provided in Chapter 02 Slides. \textbf{(6 Points)}

\item What are the values of $a_1$, $b_1$, $c_1$, $d_1$ following the convention for cubic spline interpolation formulation as described in the Chapter 02 slides? \textbf{(4 Points)}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{answerbox}

\begin{enumerate}

\item 

In the case of free boundary condition, $b_1 = 0$, $b_5 = 0$.

And

\begin{multiequation}
h_1 & = t_2 - t_1 = 1 - 0 = 1\\
h_2 & = t_3 - t_2 = 2 - 1  = 1\\
h_3 & = t_4 - t_3 = 4 - 2 = 2\\
h_4 & = t_5 - t_4 = 5 - 4 = 1
\end{multiequation}

From the lecture slides, the formula for $b_2$ to $b_n$ in matrix form is

\begin{multiequation}
\begin{bmatrix}
2\cdot(1+1) & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 2\cdot(1+2) & 2\\
0 & 2 & 2\cdot(2+1)\\
\end{bmatrix}  = \begin{bmatrix}
4  &   1  &  0\\
1  &   6  &  2\\
0  &   2  &   6
\end{bmatrix}
\end{multiequation}

If we include $b_1$ and $b_5$ in the systems of equations, $B$ matrix can be written as

\begin{multiequation}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 2\cdot(1+1) & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 2\cdot(1+2) & 2 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 2 & 2\cdot(2+1) & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}  = \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 4  &   1  &  0 & 0\\
0 & 1  &   6  &  2 & 0\\
0 & 0  &   2  &   6 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\end{multiequation}

The full systems of equations for $b_2$ to $b_4$ is given by 
 
\begin{multiequation}
\begin{bmatrix}
4  &   1  &  0\\
1  &   6  &  2\\
0  &   2  &   6
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
b_2 \\ b_3 \\ b_4
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{3(d_3-d_2)}{h_2} - \frac{3(d_2-d_1)}{h_1} \\
\frac{3(d_4-d_3)}{h_3} - \frac{3(d_3-d_2)}{h_2} \\
\frac{3(d_5-d_4)}{h_4} - \frac{3(d_4-d_3)}{h_3} 
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{3(9-7)}{1} - \frac{3(7-0)}{1} \\
\frac{3(3-9)}{2} - \frac{3(9-7)}{1} \\
\frac{3(4-3)}{1} - \frac{3(3-9)}{2}
 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
 -15\\-15\\12
 \end{bmatrix}
\end{multiequation}

which upon solving using MATLAB gives $b_2 = -3$, $b_3 = -3$, and $b_4 = 3.0$.




\item $d_1 = x_1 = 0.0$.

$c_1 = \cfrac{d_2 - d_1}{h_1} - \cfrac{1}{3}(2b_1 + b_2)h_1 = \cfrac{7-0}{1}-\cfrac{1}{3}{(-3\cdot 1)} = 7 +1 = 8$  

$a_1 = \frac{b_2 - b_1}{3h_1} = \frac{-3-0}{3\cdot 1} = -1$

and because we assumed free boundary conditions $b_1 = 0$.

\end{enumerate}


\end{answerbox}
