\item Solve the differential equation $ty'  + 3 y = 0$, given $y(1) = 2$, assuming $t > 0$. Here $y' = \frac{dy}{dt}$. \textbf{Hint 1:} you will need to rearrange terms so that derivative term is on the left hand side and non-derivative terms on the right hand side, and then integrate. \textbf{Hint 2:} $\int \frac{1}{t} dt  = \ln(t) + C$. \hfill \textbf{(5 Points)}

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    enhanced,breakable,
    colback=androidBlueLight!80,
    colframe=androidBlue,
    arc=5pt,
    boxrule=1pt,
    title=\textbf{Answer},
    fonttitle=\bfseries,
    coltitle=black,
    top=10pt,
    bottom=8pt,
    left=8pt,
    right=8pt,
    attach boxed title to top left={xshift=10pt, yshift=-\tcboxedtitleheight/2},
    boxed title style={
    colback=androidBlue,    
        colframe=androidBlue,
        arc=3pt,
        boxrule=0pt,
        left=6pt, right=6pt,
        top=3pt, bottom=3pt
    }
    ]

Rearranging the given differential equation,
\begin{align}
y' + 3\frac{y}{t} & = 0\\
\frac{dy}{dt} + 3 \frac{y}{t}  & = 0\\
\Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dt} & = - \frac{3y}{t}\\
\Rightarrow \frac{dy}{y} & = - 3 \frac{dt}{t}\\
\Rightarrow \int \frac{dy}{y} & = - 3\int \frac{dt}{t}\\
\Rightarrow \ln y & = -3 \ln| t| + C, \quad C\textbf{  is the constant of integration}\\
\Rightarrow y & = e ^{-3\ln| t| + C} =e^{-3\ln |t|} e^C
\end{align}

Let $A = e^C$, then
\begin{align*}
\Rightarrow y & = A \cdot e^{-3\ln |t|}\\
y & = At^{-3}\\
\textbf{As } y(1) & = 2 \Rightarrow 2 = A\cdot (1)^{-3} \Rightarrow A = 2\\
\textbf{Thus, } y & = 2t^{-3} 
\end{align*}
    
\end{tcolorbox}

    ]
    
From the first prinicple of the derivative

\begin{align}
f'(x) & = \lim_{h\to 0 } \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\\
& =  \lim_{h\to 0 } \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h}\\
& =  \lim_{h\to 0 } \frac{\cancel{x^2} + h ^2 + 2hx - \cancel{x^2}}{h}\\
& =  \lim_{h\to 0 } \frac{\cancel{h}(h + 2x)}{\cancel{h}}\\
& = 2x 
\end{align}    
    
\end{tcolorbox}

Nth term of a geometric progression can be written 

$a_n = a r^{n-1}$. So $a_2 = 2\cdot 3^{2-1} = 6$, $a_4 = 2\cdot 3^{4-1} = 54$, $a_7 = 2\cdot 3^{7-1} = 1458$, $a_9 = 2\cdot 3^{9-1} = 13122$.

As $|r| \not< 1$, the sum of the geometric progression doesn't converge.
